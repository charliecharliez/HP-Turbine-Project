# Turbine design project roadmap

## Purpose and use

Create a modular, iterative turbine-design program that evaluates and optimizes feasible transonic high-pressure turbine candidates. The active workflow runs from boundary conditions through meanline, row losses, airfoil geometry, selected-candidate 2D CFD, and optimization. Cooling and the AMBRA test proposal remain documented as deferred work, not prerequisites for the current optimizer end goal.

- In a future chat, name the task ID and title, review its prerequisites and discuss the approach before implementing it.
- The task order is a suggested dependency order, not a requirement to finish every item in a phase before starting another. Revisit upstream decisions when later evidence requires it.
- Each task's **Done when** describes its intended deliverable or check, not a claim that it already exists.
- The authoritative requirements are in [Project Instructions Email.pdf](./Project%20Instructions%20Email.pdf). Resolve any conflict in favor of that document.

## Active end goal

The program should take validated boundary conditions and candidate design inputs, then repeatedly evaluate and refine the design:

1. Establish the operating boundary conditions and applicable feasibility constraints.
2. Solve the meanline for the candidate design.
3. Calculate blade-row losses and feed them back into the meanline until the coupled solution converges or is explicitly reported as non-converged.
4. Generate airfoil geometry from the current flow/design state and update it as the design is refined.
5. Use 2D CFD on selected, screened candidates to check and improve the airfoil and meanline predictions, then re-evaluate the candidate.
6. Search the feasible design space and report results for three distinct modes: maximize efficiency, maximize power output, or optimize both and return the efficiency–power Pareto frontier.

All active roadmap tasks should build, verify, or integrate a part of this workflow. The candidate evaluator and optimizer must retain inputs, assumptions, convergence status, infeasible/failed candidates, warnings, and results so comparisons are reproducible. Use lower-cost meanline/loss/geometry evaluations to screen candidates; do not require a CFD solve for every optimizer iteration.

Cooling models and the AMBRA test proposal are deferred and are not blockers for this active end goal. Until cooling work is resumed, the program must not claim thermal feasibility against the metal-temperature limit or full project compliance. The 2D CFD design validation remains in scope.

## Progress snapshot

Status is assessed against each task's **Done when** criteria. **Partial** means a relevant implementation or test exists but the full deliverable or its validation is outstanding. **Not started** means no relevant implementation was found in the reviewed workflow. **Deferred** and **Optional** retain the scope choices above. No task is treated as complete merely because a prototype returns a result.

As of 2026-11-26, the current branch couples the single-stage meanline, radial-equilibrium, blade-row geometry, and Kacker–Okapuu loss modules in `src/Helpers/stage_solver.py`. It iterates loss coefficients, reports stage performance and spanwise values, and checks a limited set of uncooled constraints. `src/Helpers/turbine_solver.py` adds one- and two-stage sequential evaluation, overall work and efficiency, and first-rotor AMBRA hub-to-tip checks. `src/Helpers/design_optimizer.py` provides differential-evolution screening and full-loop verification for one- and two-stage efficiency optimization. `src/Helpers/design_sweep.py` also screens and refines single-stage efficiency candidates. This is meaningful integrated prototype progress, but it is not the required three-mode, CFD-informed optimizer.

- Shared design models validate boundary conditions, row/stage inputs, and run-record shapes; the data model can represent coolant states, while the separate sequential turbine solver provides integrated one- and two-stage meanline evaluation. Neither path provides the deferred cooling solver.

The meanline propagates local enthalpy, entropy, `cp`, `gamma`, viscosity, density, and gas constant through state transitions, radial equilibrium, airfoil geometry, and loss calculations. Coolant mixing remains deferred under C02.
- Blade-row geometry is generated at hub, midspan, and tip and passed to loss calculations. Chart loading, interpolation, selected interface behavior, velocity-triangle relations, continuity, meanline energy closure, rotor rothalpy, and pressure-based efficiency relations have focused regression tests; these do not constitute full independent validation of every physical model.

- The default stage design violates the blade-speed constraint; tests also exercise a selected single-stage design that passes the constraints currently implemented. Constraint coverage remains incomplete: cooling and metal temperature are not checked.

## C. Gas properties and deferred cooling

- **C01 - Model temperature-dependent air properties.** Evaluate cp and gamma as temperature changes, using an appropriate source; document the approximation used in each module and compare it with any constant-property assumption. **Status: Complete** — CoolProp-backed `AirState` objects now provide pressure-, temperature-, enthalpy-, and entropy-based states. The meanline, radial equilibrium, airfoil throat/Mach calculations, loss Reynolds/Mach inputs, and turbine performance use variable properties row or station by station. Regression tests verify entropy closure, pressure units, enthalpy continuity, rothalpy, Euler work, and local perfect-gas consistency. Coolant mixing remains a separate C02 scope. **Done when:** inlet, row and exit properties are reported and verified over the design range.
- **C02 - Define coolant and main-flow bookkeeping (deferred).** Decide where each row receives coolant and track coolant mass, temperature, pressure and downstream mixed flow. **Status: Deferred** — the shared data model can represent coolant states and the cooling conventions establish a 30 kg/s percentage basis, but no row-level coolant ledger or mixing implementation exists. The design reference air is 1850 K and 20 bar main flow at 30 kg/s, while compressor-delivery coolant is 750 K and 21 bar. Distinguish the rotor's hotter absolute gas temperature from its rotor-relative total gas temperature and preserve where coolant enters each stator and rotor. State the gas temperature used for each row and separate a row coolant fraction from the downstream mixed-stream fraction. **Done when:** stage and row mass/energy balances and coolant percentages relative to 30 kg/s are unambiguous.
- **C03 - Select and justify a cooling correlation (deferred).** Choose a literature correlation and define its applicability, required geometry inputs and uncertainty. **Status: Deferred** — use the project cooling references as the starting source: [ME533 cooling highlights](./src/Pipeline/Cooling/ME533_Turbine_Cooling_Highlights.pdf) summarizes the \(m^*\) method and G. Dailey's [design and calculation lecture](./src/Pipeline/Cooling/Dailey_RR_VKI-LS2000-03_Design_and_Calculation_Issues.pdf). The proposed first estimate uses cooling effectiveness \(\epsilon=(T_g-T_m)/(T_g-T_{c,in})\), cooling efficiency \(\eta_c=(T_{c,out}-T_{c,in})/(T_m-T_{c,in})\), external heat-transfer coefficient \(h_g\), wetted area \(A_g\), coolant heat capacity \(c_{p,c}\), and \(m^*= \dot m_c c_{p,c}/(h_g A_g)=\epsilon/[ \eta_c(1-\epsilon)]\). Convection alone is not sufficient for the stated goal; the report must also state any film-cooling and thermal-barrier-coating assumptions rather than substituting an unverified heat-transfer coefficient. **Done when:** a reproducible correlation and reference are documented.
- **C04 - Calculate required coolant per row (deferred).** Use cooling effectiveness and the 1250 K metal-temperature limit; use rotor-relative total gas temperature for rotors. **Status: Deferred** — the expected workflow is to identify each row's gas temperature, set \(T_m \leq 1250\) K, solve for required effectiveness and \(\eta_c\), and convert \(m^*\) into coolant mass flow with row wetted area and heat-transfer coefficient. Design for row-specific hot-gas distributions: the HP vane is the middle two-thirds of every vane under worst-temperature conditions, with 25–50% overall temperature-distribution factor (OTDF) sensitivity, while the rotor is governed by radial profile and rotor-relative temperature. Report coolant sensitivity because a 5% coolant-flow change can move metal temperature by roughly 9–15 °C and a careful estimate may carry 35–80 °C uncertainty. **Done when:** each row's coolant requirement and limiting metal temperature can be evaluated and checked.
- **C05 - Model coolant mixing and its loss (deferred).** Determine mixed states and mixing losses using consistent mass, energy and pressure-loss definitions. **Status: Deferred** — implement the main-flow/coolant mass and energy balance only after C02 is explicit. The heat-transfer model should feed its coolant outlet state, row injection temperature and pressure into the downstream state. Mix stator coolant before rotor inlet and include row injection location and flow direction. Use the Hartsel first-estimate mixing-loss expression from the cooling highlights or a documented equivalent, and account for the strong Mach-number dependence of ejection loss: leading-edge and pressure-side injection is favorable, while late suction-side ejection is much more costly. Preserve pressure, enthalpy, mass and entropy checks for each mixed state. **Done when:** zero-coolant and conservation checks pass and mixing effects are reported.
- **C06 - Quantify the cooling penalty (deferred).** Compare cooled and uncooled work, efficiencies and total coolant demand; account for coolant that bypasses the combustor. **Status: Deferred** — report total coolant as a percentage of the original 30 kg/s main flow and as any uncommitted compressor-delivery flow. Use the heated-gas-to-coolant mass-energy effects, row mixing losses, coolant ejection work loss and compressor extraction penalty consistently; do not treat coolant as free work or as a change in the main-flow mass-flow basis. The historical design context in the highlights gives indicative ranges of 9–12% core flow for an HP vane and 3–4% for an HP blade, but these are not acceptance targets for this project. Compare cooled and uncooled work, total-to-total efficiency and total-to-static efficiency with explicit assumptions. **Done when:** the impact and assumptions are visible for each design candidate.

## Key dependencies and decision points

- Requirements, conventions, shared models, engineering-invariant tests, gas-property unit conversion, loss-chart loading and single-stage hardware checks establish foundations. The A05 run-record path currently has a test-blocking field-name mismatch, and downstream constraints remain incomplete. Physics audits **B04-B06** should inform implementation.
- Candidate evaluation depends on validated boundary conditions and gas properties (**A05**, **B04**, **B05**, **B06**, **C01**), then one- or two-stage meanline and row losses (**D02-D05**, **E01-E03**) coupled with actual row geometry (**G01-G06**). Spanwise design (**F**) informs hub, midspan and tip constraints and geometry.
- Optimization (**E04-E05**) follows the validated candidate evaluator. It compares stage count and other design variables in the three objective modes; selected candidates proceed to 2D CFD (**H**) and CFD-informed refinement before final comparison.
- Integrated reporting and verification (**J01-J05**) depend on traceable candidate runs and validated model outputs. Cooling (**C02-C06**) and the AMBRA proposal (**I01-I05**) are deferred and are not dependencies of the active optimizer workflow.
- Decisions such as cooling correlation, CFD solver, vortex law, and optimizer implementation details remain for their dedicated tasks.
